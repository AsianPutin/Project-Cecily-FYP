Introduction
Long-distance relationship (LDR) couples currently face significant "Information Fragmentation," having to jump between multiple high-maintenance apps like Life360 for location, airline apps for flight status, and notes for shared lists. Furthermore, many dedicated couple apps (e.g., Couple Tree) prioritize "digital fluff"—virtual pets, furnished gardens, or daily emotional "essays" that require users to scrape the "bottom of their brain barrels" for deep thoughts every morning.
While one partner may enjoy these inefficiencies, the other—often the logical or forgetful type—finds these forced interactions mentally draining. There is a distinct lack of a utility-first LDR hub that automates logistics without demanding constant, active emotional labor or high battery consumption. 
This project proposes an Android-native utility system that centralizes logistics—including airline-grade flight tracking, health cycle syncing (inspired by Blood), and shared wishlists (inspired by Elfster)—into a low-friction dashboard. It prioritizes passive "Ambient Awareness" through home-screen widgets and automated status updates, allowing partners to stay connected through practical data rather than repetitive gamification.
Problem Statements
Friction of Daily Access & Emotional Fatigue
Existing apps often require manual daily logins and forced deep thinking prompts to maintain streaks. For logical or busy users, these tasks feel like digital chores, leading to app abandonment when they lack the mental bandwidth for journaling. 
Feature Bloat & Financial Pressure  
Competitors often focus on virtual pets and house-furnishing leaderboards that become predictable and boring. These systems frequently pressure users into spending real money on tokens or subscriptions to progress, distracting from the actual purpose of relationship maintenance.  
Logistical Fragmentation & Battery Drain 
There is no unified, low-power solution for LDR logistics. Couples currently jump between fragmented platforms, and GPS-heavy trackers like Life360 are notoriously battery-intensive, lacking a "Together Mode" to disable tracking when the couple is physically in the same location. 
Objectives
Quick and easy pairing and syncing 
To develop a secure mobile application module that enables real-time synchronization between two devices through a unique 6-digit verification token pairing mechanism. 
Flight tracker within the app 
To integrate a background travel monitoring framework that processes pasted flight telemetry data to display real-time status updates and arrival estimations passively to the partner. 
Integrated lifestyle hub 
To design a centralized dashboard that enables users to coordinate shared calendar events, manage lifestyle wishlists with post-purchase privacy shielding, and manually log health cycles.
Decision helper 
To implement a preference-shielded activity selection utility using a swipe-based interface to facilitate joint decision-making without real-time choice bias.
Dynamic ambient widgets 
To deploy passive home-screen desktop widgets that provide glanceable status indicators, including distance metrics, photo cycling, and a single-button notification ping for ambient awareness.
Scope

Couples in LDRs where one partner seeks logistical automation (the "logical" type) and the other seeks consistent updates. 
System Scope
An Android-native application (API 30+) using Firebase for real-time syncing. The system integrates external Aviation APIs and prioritizes low-power background tasks to protect battery life.

Background Study
Differentiating Dating (Matchmaking) Apps from Couple Management Apps 
A recurring source of confusion among the general audience is the difference between Dating Applications and Couple Management Applications. While both of these platforms are used by dating and married couples, they have virtually opposite target audiences, user data structures, and core loop functions. 
Dating (Matchmaking) Applications: Dating applications, which include Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, and other similar products, are all focused on finding and approaching strangers. Their core loop is based on discovering, swiping, and chatting with potential matches via short-text messages. Meanwhile, couple-focused programs such as Between, Couple Tree, and Project Cecily enable two users to build a closed, one-to-one network within the app. There are no third parties, relationship feeds or search systems in couple management applications. After two sides successfully pass an invite code, a one-to-one encrypted chatroom appears for two-sided synchronization. 
Couple Management Applications: The research conducted by Jiang and Hancock (2013) showed that asynchronous digital communication channels could effectively recreate the intimacy of a physical relationship, provided that both sides share sufficiently sensitive, personally meaningful information. Li et al. (2022) note that in terms of maintaining long-distance relationships, one-to-two social tools present the best possible approximation of a married couple’s social support system. Thus, it may be said that these couple management tools enable intimate digital conversations between partners. 
The fundamental difference between the two types of dating apps is essential to understanding what functions couple-oriented software such as Project Cecily is capable of. The latter lacks any of the public discovery-oriented features of Tinder, Bumble, or other platforms mentioned above. 
Adoption of LDR Apps and the Friction of "Gamification" 
Long-distance relationships are relying more and more on mobile applications to keep their LDRs close. But most LDR software today focuses too much on gamification. Applications have virtual pets, furnished gardens, avatar dressing and journaling prompts or diary streaks which users are compelled to update.
While gamification can be fun at first, studies on Human-Computer Interaction show that it has limits to its effectiveness, especially in daily relationship maintenance.
Interaction Fatigue: Holtzman et al. (2021) found that the association between relationship satisfaction for LDRs who used text messages and other communication apps for interacting was strong. But when it came to compulsory electronic communication, the tendency was that maintaining a long-distance relationship became a “digital chore.” People who use relationship maintenance mobile applications are usually tired and busy; they don’t always want to write emotional essays or do diary prompts to refresh a streak. 
Asymmetric Engagement: For instance, one partner may find virtual pets and gardens amusing while the other sees it as a waste of time. Thus the partner who has the “practical eye” is put in the uncomfortable position of choosing between virtual games or maintaining a good LDR. 
Passive Connection through "Ambient Awareness" 
Ambient awareness is the feeling of presence of another person or people around you. It provides a daily source of practical information on what the other person is doing without necessitating interaction or conversation. Automatic flight tracking, health or cycle tracking, and desktop widgets are examples of ambient awareness features. Watkins et al. (2011) showed that people were happier when they had access to current information. Their work demonstrated that transitively aware people had lesser stress while on the move or waiting. When it comes to the application of ambient awareness in long-distance relationships, Pöhler et al. (2024) explain the value of exchanging “physical” vectors or indicators as proxies for being present with another person. In addition to helping people feel closer to each other despite the distance, these ambient awareness features reduce cognitive load. In countries that have made digital transformation blueprint documents like the Sarawak Digital Economy Blueprint 2030 (Sarawak Digital Economy Corporation, 2022), ambient awareness features would facilitate the achievement of the vision through utilitarian apps that average citizens would easily adopt. The Project Cecily app uses ambient awareness to develop an automated application for couples in long-distance relationships. 
Overcoming Decision Fatigue: Weighted Consensus Swiping 
When an LDR couple finally meets or plans to meet, they tend to face “decision paralysis” on where to go or what to do. Taking inspiration from the quick swipe feature common in discovery mobile applications, project Cecily would implement the swiping gestures in a closed 1-to-1 session for decision-making purposes. This allows the couple to anonymously vote for their preferred choice (e.g., food or activity) and reach a consensus. 
Commercial Revenue Models and User Experience Friction in Mobile Applications 
A critical factor influencing long-term user retention in relationship technology is the economic design of the platform. Commercial couple applications employ several monetization strategies, each creating distinct operational and psychological trade-offs: 
Freemium and In-App Advertising: Ad-supported freemium models display banner ads or interstitial video pop-ups during user interactions. In intimate relationship tools, unsolicited advertisements intrude upon private communication spaces, inducing digital fatigue and creating a perception of commercial surveillance.
Impulse Subscriptions and Emotional Paywalls: Platforms frequently restrict core functionality—such as historical cycle tracking, custom desktop home widgets, or unlimited preference swipes—behind recurring monthly or annual subscription tiers. These "emotional paywalls" capitalize on impulsive subscription sign-ups during initial relationship milestones. However, recurring paywalls create financial friction when partners reside in different economic regions or operate under student budgets.
One-Time "Freedom" Purchases: As an alternative to recurring subscriptions, one-time lifetime access fees remove paywalls permanently. Software ergonomics research indicates that users express higher trust and lower churn rates when software is offered under transparent, non-recurring models that preserve full access to core logistics.
Micro-Donation and Creator Support Mechanisms (e.g., Ko-fi Integration): Voluntary micro-contribution protocols allow users to financially support developer server costs without locking essential features behind paywalls. By leveraging external micro-donation links (such as Ko-fi), personal utility applications maintain an open baseline while providing an optional, non-coercive financial support channel. 
E-Commerce Affiliate Link Redirection: Standalone wishlist platforms monetized via affiliate marketing automatically intercept and rewrite user-submitted store URLs to append tracking parameters. While generating platform commission, affiliate URL tampering introduces security risks, tracking script bloat, and broken deep-links when opening regional mobile shopping applications. 
Understanding these commercial models demonstrates why the proposed project adopts an "Open-Baseline Utility Strategy." By delivering all core operational features (flight tracking, shared health/cycle status, decision swiping, and desktop widgets) without paywalls or forced subscriptions, the system eliminates financial pressure between partners while supporting non-intrusive voluntary contributions.

Regional Transport Utilities (e.g., MYairports): Couples traveling to visit each other check flight status via airport portals like MYairports (MAHB, 2023). They lack a safe, bi-directional 1-to-1 pair sync mode that allows a remote partner to provide proactive emotional support based on synchronized cycle phases.

Standalone Wishlist & Gift Exchange Platforms: Giftlist vs. Elfster 
When planning reunions, holidays, or gift exchanges across geographical distances, couples often turn to dedicated gift registry platforms to manage wishlists: 
Giftlist: Designed as a general gift registry platform allowing users to aggregate items they want from various online stores. Its key feature is surprise preservation: when a partner or friend buys an item from the list, they can mark it as "reserved" or "purchased." The platform updates the item status for everyone viewing the list while intentionally hiding the reservation status from the list owner. While this prevents duplicate purchases while keeping gifts a surprise, Giftlist operates as a completely disconnected single-purpose utility. It lacks relationship dashboard integration, real-time messaging, or transit synchronization.

Elfster: Primarily built for group "Secret Santa" gift exchanges. It pairs participants randomly and provides anonymous wishlist sharing. While Elfster handles gift coordination well for large families or workplaces, applying it to a strict 1-to-1 LDR couple dynamic highlights a distinct functional mismatch: in a 2-person relationship, the "Secret Santa" assignment is redundant, yet couples still use it because standalone couple apps lack native wishlist management.
To resolve this, the proposed system incorporates a Surprise-Preserving Shared Wishlist natively into the 1-to-1 couple dashboard. When Partner A adds a gift idea, place to visit, or reunion goal, Partner B can mark it as reserved or completed in Firebase Firestore. The database state updates for Partner B without revealing the reservation state to Partner A, blending the utility of Giftlist and Elfster directly into the daily LDR workflow.

Critique of Traditional (Waterfall) Methodology
Selecting an appropriate software development lifecycle (SDLC) model is critical to project feasibility and risk management. Traditional development models, such as the Waterfall model, follow a strictly linear and sequential path: system requirements must be fully locked before architectural design begins, implementation occurs in a single phase, and verification occurs only after the complete codebase is written.  
While Waterfall offers predictability in projects with static, well-understood parameters, it presents severe structural vulnerabilities for modern mobile application development: 
Inflexible Requirement Freezing: In Waterfall, modifying system requirements once implementation has commenced requires costly retrofitting. Mobile software relying on third-party real-time integrations—such as external Aviation Telemetry APIs, Google Maps SDK, and WebSocket database listeners—operates under fluid technical boundaries. If an API endpoint schema changes or an OS background execution policy is updated, a Waterfall workflow struggles to adapt without invalidating previously approved design artifacts.
Deferred Integration Testing: In a sequential lifecycle, complete module integration occurs late in the project timeline. For a system featuring interdependent subsystems (such as native Java home widgets querying Firebase Firestore state changes triggered by background flight parsers), discovering inter-module communication failures during late-stage verification poses catastrophic schedule risks.
Delayed Stakeholder Feedback: Verification occurs at the end of the development cycle. Consequently, user interface flaws or usability issues are discovered when resolving them requires extensive code rewrites
As Al-Saqqa et al. (2020) observe, rigid linear methodologies account for a significant proportion of software project delays and budget overruns when applied to dynamic mobile environments. Therefore, the traditional Waterfall approach was deemed unsuitable for this project. 
Defense of Agile Software Development
To overcome the failure modes of linear methodologies, the software engineering discipline heavily favors Agile Software Development. Agile paradigms decompose system development into short, time-boxed iterative cycles known as sprints. Each sprint yields a working, testable increment of the application, transforming project delivery into a progressive evolution rather than a single high-risk deployment.  
Agile offers distinct operational advantages for mobile utility platforms:  
Adaptive Requirement Handling: Rather than locking requirements upfront, Agile treats user stories as evolving specifications. Requirements for real-time flight telemetry, cycle tracking synchronization, and decision swiping can be refined incrementally based on empirical testing.
Early and Continuous Risk Reduction: By deploying functional increments at the conclusion of each sprint, technical risks—such as Firestore WebSocket latency or widget update performance—are identified and resolved early in the timeline.
Continuous Stakeholder Validation: Bi-weekly reviews with the project supervisor allow working software to be demonstrated continuously. Feedback is incorporated immediately into the upcoming sprint backlog rather than waiting for a final project defense.
In an extensive empirical evaluation of project management frameworks, Gemino et al. (2021) demonstrated that Agile approaches achieve statistically higher rates of project success, stakeholder satisfaction, and code quality compared to rigid Waterfall implementations, particularly in projects managed under constrained schedules 
Tailoring Agile and Extreme Programming (XP) for a Solo Developer 
While standard Agile frameworks such as Scrum are designed for multi-disciplinary teams requiring daily stand-up meetings, dedicated Scrum Masters, and formal Product Owners, applying pure Scrum to a solo developer project introduces unnecessary administrative overhead.
To maximize engineering efficiency without sacrificing rigor, this project adopts a hybrid Agile/Extreme Programming (XP) strategy specifically tailored for single-developer execution (Beck et al., 2001; Day, 2022):

This prevents logic bugs from propagating into the presentation layer.
Continuous Integration & Refactoring: Source code is managed via GitHub version control, with feature branches merged systematically to maintain a stable main branch. Refactoring is practiced continuously to keep the native Java codebase simple and modular.
By combining Scrum's structured sprint cadence with XP's developer-centric engineering practices, the methodology provides a robust, low-overhead framework designed to deliver a fully functional, tested native Android platform within the allocated 16-week timeline.

Frontend Development: Android Native (Java ) vs. Cross-Platform 
While cross-platform frameworks like Flutter or React Native allow simultaneous iOS and Android deployment, native Android development using Java and Android Studio (Android Developers, 2023) was selected for Project Cecily due to core platform compatibility:
Native Interactive Home Widgets (AppWidgetProvider): Project Cecily relies heavily on home-screen ambient widgets. Native Java APIs offer straightforward access to Android's RemoteViews and AppWidgetManager classes, providing robust power management and immediate gesture responsiveness for interactive desktop widgets (such as tapping a button to "poke" a partner or display flight countdowns) without cross-platform bridge overhead.
Stable SDK Lifecycle Management: Developing in Java ensures deep compatibility with legacy and modern Android SDK versions, facilitating reliable background event handling and system broadcast listeners.
Backend & Real-Time Sync: Firebase Firestore 
Firebase Firestore (Firebase, 2023) was chosen as the cloud database due to its NoSQL document structure and real-time WebSocket capabilities. When one partner updates a wishlist, logs a cycle event, sends a widget poke, or completes a decision swipe, Firestore pushes the change to the partner's device instantly via Java listener adapters without requiring manual screen refreshes. 
Logistics, Mapping & Decision Infrastructure 
To eliminate manual flight checking, Project Cecily uses background flight tracking. The system parses pasted flight numbers, queries external Aviation Telemetry APIs, and updates status fields in Firestore in real time. Google Maps SDK for Android (Google Maps Platform, 2023) provides mapping and location visualization for transit coordination. Additionally, the consensus resolver stores temporary swipe vectors in Firestore, matching overlapping "YES" swipes for dates, food, or vacation ideas to return an instant match to both devices. 
CONCLUSION 
The literature and market review reveals a clear gap in relationship software. Existing couple applications focus heavily on gamified tasks, virtual pets, and daily streak maintenance, which often lead to digital burnout and friction for logical or busy users. Conversely, functional tools like Life360, airport portals, and health apps (Blood, Flo) treat users as isolated individuals, forcing LDR couples to jump between multiple disjointed platforms to manage their shared lives. 
Project Cecily bridges this gap by combining real-time flight telemetry, integrated cycle awareness, consensus decision swiping, and interactive ambient widgets into a unified, low-power Android application. The technical stack—Java, Android Studio, Firebase Firestore, Google Maps SDK, and Aviation APIs—provides the precise native capabilities required to deliver this solution efficiently.

Sprint Structure 
The project schedule spans a total of 16 weeks, running from August to the end of November. Development is broken into four distinct 4-week sprints. Each sprint follows a consistent structure drawn from XP and Scrum practices:
Sprint Planning: Define sprint goals, break user stories into concrete development tasks, and set acceptance criteria.
Development & Execution: Write native Java code in Android Studio, integrate Firebase Firestore, apply test-first development, and commit code regularly to GitHub version control.
Sprint Review (Supervisor Consultation): Demonstrate working increments to the project supervisor to validate progress against project objectives.
Sprint Retrospective: Evaluate blockers, refine code structure, and re-prioritize the product backlog for the next sprint.

Use Case Scenarios
UC-01: Authenticate & Pair Devices 
Actor: User (Partner A), Paired Partner (Partner B)
Precondition: App installed on both Android devices; internet connection available.
Postcondition: Users authenticated and linked in a 1-to-1 pair collection in Firebase Firestore.
Trigger: User opens app and selects "Create Pair Code" or "Enter Invite Code".
Main Success Scenario:
Partner A signs up via Firebase Auth and generates a 6-digit pair code.
Partner B opens the app, selects "Join Pair", and enters the code.
The system validates code in Firestore and links the two user IDs.
Both users are redirected to the shared main dashboard.
Extensions:
1a. Invalid or expired code: System displays "Code invalid" error.
UC-02: Track Flight Logistics 
Actor: User
Precondition: User is paired; valid flight code is available.
Postcondition: Flight status monitored; updates displayed on user and partner screens.
Trigger: User pastes or enters a flight number (e.g., "MH2712").
Main Success Scenario:
The user pastes the flight number into the travel module.
System parses string and queries Aviation Telemetry API.
System updates Firestore with departure time, gate, and estimated arrival.
Partner’s device receives Firestore push update and updates map/card view.
Extensions:
1a. API unavailable: System displays last saved status with warning.
UC-03: Share Health & Cycle Log 
Actor: User (Female Partner)
Precondition: Users are paired.
Postcondition: Cycle status logged and securely shared in a pair workspace.
Trigger: User logs start/end date or symptoms in Health Hub.
Main Success Scenario:
The user selects cycle dates in the calendar.
The system computes phase (e.g., Ovulation, Luteal) and saves to Firestore pair record.
Partner’s app fetches updates and shows passive mood/support tips.
UC-04: Resolve Decision via Swiper 
Actor: User (Partner A), Paired Partner (Partner B)
Precondition: Both partners are paired; active session started for "Food" or "Dates".
Postcondition: Consensus match calculated and displayed to both users.
Trigger: User initiates a decision session.
Main Success Scenario:
The user starts a swiping session (e.g., "Dinner Options in Kuching").
Users swipe Left (No) or Right (Yes) on options.
System records swipe choices in Firestore.
When both users swipe "Yes" on the same item, the system calculates a match and triggers a "Match Found!" screen on both devices.
UC-05: Send Widget Poke / Alert 
Actor: User
Precondition: Home widget added to Android home screen; devices paired.
Postcondition: Poke counter incremented and vibration/toast alert triggered on partner device.
Trigger: User taps "Poke" button on desktop widget.
Main Success Scenario:
The user taps the "Poke" button on the home screen widget.
AppWidgetProvider triggers background update to Firestore.
The partner's device receives a push alert and updates the partner widget UI.
UC-06: View Live Location Map
Actor: User, Paired Partner
Precondition: Google Maps SDK configured; location permissions granted.
Postcondition: Location coordinates and airport markers displayed on map.
Trigger: User navigates to Location tab.
Main Success Scenario:
User opens Location view.
App reads current coordinates and fetches partner's last known location from Firestore.
Google Maps view renders both markers with distance calculation.
UC-07: Manage Shared Wishlist
Actor: User, Paired Partner
Precondition: Pair connection active.
Postcondition: Shared item added, updated, or checked off in Firestore.
Trigger: User adds a place to visit, gift idea, or travel goal.
Main Success Scenario:
The user enters the item title, category, and notes into the Wishlist tab.
App writes new entry to pair's Firestore collection.
Partner's device receives real-time listener update and refreshes wishlist UI list.
