Title: Couple’s Companion App (Temporary name)
	Introduction:
Long-distance relationship (LDR) couples currently face significant "Information Fragmentation," having to jump between multiple high-maintenance apps like Life360 for location, airline apps for flight status, and notes for shared lists. Furthermore, many dedicated couple apps (e.g., Couple Tree) prioritize "digital fluff"—virtual pets, furnished gardens, or daily emotional "essays" that require users to scrape the "bottom of their brain barrels" for deep thoughts every morning.
While one partner may enjoy these inefficiencies, the other—often the logical or forgetful type—finds these forced interactions mentally draining. There is a distinct lack of a utility-first LDR hub that automates logistics without demanding constant, active emotional labor or high battery consumption. 
This project proposes an Android-native utility system that centralizes logistics—including airline-grade flight tracking, health cycle syncing (inspired by Blood), and shared wishlists (inspired by Elfster)—into a low-friction dashboard. It prioritizes passive "Ambient Awareness" through home-screen widgets and automated status updates, allowing partners to stay connected through practical data rather than repetitive gamification.
	Problem Statements:
1. Friction of Access & Forced Deep Thinking: 
Existing apps often require manual daily logins and forced deep thinking prompts to maintain streaks. For logical or busy users, these tasks feel like digital chores, leading to app abandonment when they lack the mental bandwidth for journaling. 
2. Feature Bloat & Financial Pressure: 
Competitors often focus on virtual pets and house-furnishing leaderboards that become predictable and boring. These systems frequently pressure users into spending real money on tokens or subscriptions to progress, distracting from the actual purpose of relationship maintenance. 
3. Logistical Fragmentation & Battery Drain: 
There is no unified, low-power solution for LDR logistics. Couples currently jump between fragmented platforms, and GPS-heavy trackers like Life360 are notoriously battery-intensive, lacking a "Together Mode" to disable tracking when the couple is physically in the same location. 
	Objectives:
   1. Secure Handshake & Pair Sync: 
Develop a Firebase-backed pairing protocol using 6-digit invite codes to link two users and manage real-time data persistence for shared profiles. 
   2. Automated Flight Logistics Engine: 
Implement a background monitoring service using WorkManager that automatically tracks flight status (takeoff, landing, delays) from pasted flight data to provide ETA alerts to the partner. 
   3. Integrated Lifestyle & Health Hub: 
Build a centralized dashboard for shared wishlists and period tracking to automate support and visit planning without daily manual entry. 
   4. Weighted Consensus Resolver: 
Implement a Tinder-style swiping mechanic for date and food preferences that uses pre-existing preferences to help the couple decide on activities when together. 
   5. Multi-State Ambient Widgets: 
Develop a suite of Android home-screen widgets for passive connection, including random photo cycles, a Poke interaction button, and a low-power proximity tracker that auto-disables when partners are together. 
	Scope:
User Scope:
      * Couples in LDRs where one partner seeks logistical automation (the "logical" type) and the other seeks consistent updates. 
System Scope:
      * An Android-native application (API 30+) using Firebase for real-time syncing. The system integrates external Aviation APIs and prioritizes low-power background tasks to protect battery life. 
	Revenue Model:
The app will use a multi-tiered monetization strategy to attract impulsive buyers while remaining student-friendly:
      * Freemium/Ads: The app is free with non-intrusive banner ads.
      * Impulse Subscription: A low-cost monthly tier ($0.99) to remove ads and unlock power features. 
      * One-Time "Freedom" Purchase: A single payment option (equivalent to 10 months of subscription) to unlock everything forever. 
      * Ko-fi Function: An optional "funsies" donation button for users who wish to support the developer's work with no additional features attached.
	Methodology:
The Agile model in the software development life cycle will be used to develop the application. Agile is an adaptable and iterative approach that emphasizes teamwork and producing usable software in small steps through time-boxed iterations and continuous feedback. This model is chosen to allow for the developer to manage this project alongside other subjects, an internship, and personal testing with their partner. By developing in incremental, rapid cycles, the project can maintain a flexible baseline, ensuring requirement satisfaction and a functional delivery by December 2026.
	Software Tools:
      1. IDE: Android Studio
      2. Backend: Firebase (Firestore, Auth, Cloud Functions). 
      3. Documentation: Google Office Suite (Docs, Sheets).
      4. Hardware: Two physical Android devices and a development laptop.
	Conclusion:
This project prioritizes logical utility over the "forced deep thinking" typical of current couple apps. By centralizing flight logistics, health tracking, and decision-making into a low-friction Android system, it creates a practical bridge between partners. The Agile methodology ensures the project remains manageable across two semesters, delivering a high-quality, privacy-focused tool that respects the user's battery life and mental bandwidth.
